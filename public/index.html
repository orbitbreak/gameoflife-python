Conway’s Game of Life

Created by mathematician John Conway in 1970, this cellular automaton became a classic example of how complex behavior can emerge from a few simple local rules.

Each cell looks at its eight neighbors. A dead cell is born with exactly three live neighbors, while a live cell survives with two or three. All other cells die or stay empty.

How Life works

Each generation is computed all at once. A live cell survives with two or three live neighbors. A dead cell is born with exactly three. Every other cell is empty next time.

Press Space from the page to play or pause. When the grid is focused, Space instead toggles the selected cell; focused controls keep their normal keyboard behavior.

Finite edges treat everything beyond the frame as dead. Wrapping edges connect opposite sides into a torus.

The Time Machine keeps the latest 240 states. Scrub backward and then edit or step to discard the abandoned future and begin a new branch.
